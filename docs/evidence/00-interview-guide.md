# Interview Guide

**Method:** semi-structured needs interviews, 15 to 20 minutes, in person or online.
**Participants:** 3 to 5 parents of children aged 0 to 5 (codes P1 to P5) and 1 to 2 specialists (codes SP1, SP2).
**Purpose:** test assumptions A1 to A8 (document 02, section 2.4). These are not usability tests; the prototype is not shown.
**Notes:** written into the templates in [interview-notes.md](interview-notes.md).

## Consent script (read aloud)

> We are students of Astana IT University working on a diploma project about a web system in which parents keep a record of a young child's growth and development. We would like to ask you some questions for about 15 minutes. Taking part is voluntary, you can skip any question and stop at any time. We do not record audio or video. We write short notes without your name, contacts or your child's name, and refer to you only by a code such as P1. The notes are used only for this project. Do you agree to take part?

Record the answer as "yes" or "no" in the notes. If the answer is "no", do not continue.

## Note-taking rules

- Write the participant code, date, format (in person or online), duration and interviewer.
- Do not write names, contacts, addresses, the child's name or birth date, or the name of the clinic or kindergarten. Write the child's age in years and months only.
- Write short summaries of what was said; quote only short phrases, and only exactly as said.
- Mark clearly what is the participant's statement and what is the interviewer's interpretation.
- Fill in the "Supports / contradicts" line after the interview, based on the notes only.
- Commit the notes within one day of the interview.

## Questions for parents

1. How old is your child? Which measurements do you know (weight, height, head circumference), and where do you keep them?
2. After a polyclinic visit, do you receive the measured values? Can you find last year's values within a minute?
3. Have you ever compared your child's growth with a chart or a norm? How did you do it?
4. How do you know which new skills to expect at your child's age? Have you heard of milestone checklists?
5. Has anyone else (a speech therapist, a psychologist, a teacher) written about your child? How did you learn about it?
6. Would you want a specialist to see your records? What would you want to control?
7. What worries you about storing these records in an app? Would you accept a message such as "discuss this with your pediatrician"?
8. What would make you stop using such an app?

## Questions for specialists

1. Which records do you keep about a child, and how do you receive information from the family?
2. Would a parent's record of measurements and milestone answers help you? Which parts would you trust, and which would you check yourself?
3. Would you write notes in a shared record if the parent controls access?
4. Which wording of signals would you consider safe for parents?

## Follow-up questions

| Code | Question | Asked to | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| FU1 | Read one milestone item aloud in Russian (or Kazakh): "Can you say in your own words what the child should be able to do?" | Parents | A8 |
| FU2 | "Would you like to read specialist notes in full or as a short summary? How often would you add observations yourself: daily, weekly, after a visit?" | Parents | A3, quick-entry mode (document 08, 8.8) |
| FU3 | "Would you rather use a website on the phone, added to the home screen, or an app from the store? Why?" | Parents | A5 |

Additional follow-ups for specialists:

- "Would you want to write notes for several children at once, for example after a group session?"
- "On a growth chart, which lines are easier to explain to parents: percentile lines or z-score lines?"
